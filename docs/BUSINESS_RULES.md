# Workforce business rules

## Store membership and owners

Each location owns a separate workplace database. IDs, usernames, staff email addresses, schedules, chat channels, images, financial records, and audits belong to that selected store. A hub owner can create and enter its own locations through My stores but cannot enter another owner's locations or read the legacy hub workplace. Store-owner entry creates an administrator session only in the selected location; the owner role itself is a restricted hub role.

Store creation asks for business name and location, with a visible custom store code and a Generate code button. All new codes contain exactly six ASCII digits; leading zeros are preserved. Generated codes are random numbers from `000000` through `999999`, checked for availability with bounded retries. Leaving the code blank generates it on the server. A database uniqueness constraint also prevents simultaneous requests from reserving the same code. Duplicate custom codes retain the submitted form and request another code. Existing codes and links are unchanged. Time zone remains optional. A code identifies a location for public store search and sign-in; it does not grant membership. Staff registration inside a store requires an invitation from its administrator, executive or HR account, tied to an existing active employee profile. Acceptance creates employee access and consumes eligibility for that invitation; expiration is seven days. Owners and administrators assign employee, manager, HR, or payroll access through Staff access with their password and recent MFA when enabled. Platform/owner roles still require trusted server administration; reserved accounts and the actor's own role cannot be changed through this form. Public search displays business/location labels and sign-in links only. Channels are shared by the active coworkers of their location.

## Timesheets, workweeks, and reporting periods

`PayrollRecord` is one daily total per employee/work date, representing all shifts on that date. Hours are decimal values with two places, between 0 and 24. Saving a repeated employee/date is an explicit correction of that total. Notes are bounded to 2,000 characters. Future work dates are rejected.

`Workweek` is Monday–Sunday. In date order, the first 40 hours use the saved regular rate; subsequent hours use the saved overtime multiplier. `PayPeriod` is an inclusive reporting interval with a start on/before its end. Reporting periods can cover a partial week without changing the workweek allocation. Weekly reports are week-to-date, monthly reports are month-to-date, and last-30-day reports include today plus the preceding 29 days.

Daily money is rounded to cents using `Decimal` and `ROUND_HALF_UP`. Rates/multipliers are saved on submission, so later employee compensation changes do not retroactively change prior timesheets. APIs serialize monetary values/rates as strings to preserve decimal representation; hour values remain JSON numbers.

The lifecycle is Pending, Reviewed, Approved, Rejected, or Voided. Authorized reviewers may review/approve/reject another employee's Pending or Reviewed entry. Employees cannot correct an approved entry. Authorized payroll staff can correct it, which resets the corrected entry and any changed weekly allocations to Pending and clears review metadata. Rejected and voided entries are excluded from active workweek allocation and reports. Voiding preserves the source row. Resubmitting a rejected date makes it Pending again.

Pending and reviewed entries shown in reports are **estimates**, not completed disbursements. Taxes, deductions, benefits, statutory daily overtime, holiday premiums, leave pay, jurisdiction-specific workweek rules, rounding policies required by law, and bank payments are outside the implemented model. Do not claim legal payroll compliance without jurisdiction-specific review.

## Attendance

Every linked account can use **Your shift** on its dashboard to clock in and out of its own shift. The status changes from Off shift to On shift and shows the start time in the configured timezone. The personal clock always uses the signed-in employee identity, including for management roles; submitted employee IDs cannot target someone else. Management attendance permissions remain separate.

Clock events use server time in UTC. The database stores naive UTC datetimes; the UI shows the configured IANA timezone and records the clock-in date in that timezone. API timestamps include an explicit UTC offset. Offset-aware corrections are normalized to UTC. Duplicate active shifts are prohibited by a partial unique index, including under concurrent insert attempts.

Clock-out must follow clock-in, a completed shift cannot be completed again, and a shift cannot exceed 24 hours. Overnight shifts are allowed within that limit. Attendance does not write payroll automatically; users submit reviewed daily timesheet totals separately and allocate overnight work to the applicable dates.

Trusted server operators can correct a stale/incorrect shift without deleting its history:

```text
python -m flask --app run correct-attendance RECORD_ID --clock-in 2026-10-05T10:00:00-04:00 --clock-out 2026-10-05T18:00:00-04:00
```

Both timestamps require explicit offsets. The CLI logs old/new times. Payroll totals remain unchanged until their separate correction/review workflow runs.

## Leave, privacy, and record retention

Leave requires a recognized request type and a start on/before its end. Pending or approved requests for the same employee cannot overlap, including touching dates. Application validation explains the error; SQLite triggers enforce it under concurrent writes. Rejected dates may be requested again. Only Pending leave may be reviewed, and no user can review their own request. Optimistic version checks reject stale reviews.

Notifications are stored/delivered to the intended in-app user, and read/mark-all actions are scoped to that same owner for every role. Roles do not grant access to another user's private inbox.

Employee archival preserves user, timesheet, attendance, leave, and audit records and disables the user's login/session access. Self-archival is refused, and an active shift must be completed first. Financial records are voided rather than deleted. Audit logs include workflow/security events and payroll correction hours; they are not an external, immutable audit service.

Management read access is intentionally organization-wide in this version. Department-level manager permissions, automated shift allocation, and configurable legal payroll policies require additional design and validation.
# Workplace schedule, corrections, and chat

The Today page presents only the signed-in employee's clock and upcoming dated shifts. Coworker conversations live on Team chat. Management controls are under Manage workplace. Managers, HR, administrators, and executives may add or correct punches and create, edit, or cancel schedules; payroll has read access. Employees read only their own dated shifts.

Browser punch corrections require a reason and retain the same record ID, with audited old/new times and hours. Entire missed shifts can also be added. Actual punches cannot be future timestamps or overlap another attendance record, and completed shifts must last more than zero and no more than 24 hours. An empty clock-out keeps the shift active. Corrections do not update payroll automatically. Optimistic versions reject stale forms and SQLite triggers prevent overlapping shift writes.

Schedules use actual dates and UTC storage with configured local-time inputs/display. They may cross midnight but must last no more than 24 hours. Cancellation retains the record. Cancelled shifts disappear from employee schedules, and the cancellation is audited. Profile typical-hours text is separate from the dated schedule. Local times that are ambiguous or nonexistent during daylight-saving changes are rejected.

The weekly board uses local Monday–Sunday boundaries and allocates overnight hours to each actual calendar day, clipping at week boundaries and accounting for clock changes. Weekly shift counts count each record once even if its card continues on another day. Management can filter the selected store's employees; personal views ignore attempts to select another employee and expose only that account's shifts/time off. The weekly query does not silently truncate after 500 shifts.

The schedule form takes an employee, explicit day selections from the displayed week, and one pair of local clock times. At least one day is required; up to seven selected days are saved, with duplicate selections deduplicated and no implicit extra date. An end time earlier than the start means the following morning. Equal clock times are rejected for new shifts. Existing full-day shifts retain their next-day end when edited without changing their hours, including across clock changes; the actual 24-hour limit still applies. Notes are optional and collapsed by default. The server builds all dates and validates them even without JavaScript. The legacy datetime/repeat input remains accepted for compatibility and includes its original start date. All intervals, overlap checks, and approved-leave checks pass before any row is inserted. All shifts and per-shift audits commit together; a database conflict rolls the group back. Editing changes one versioned shift and cannot silently create repetitions. A failed edit retains its record ID and submitted version, selected date/days, times, and notes.

Approved leave covers inclusive local dates. Any scheduled interval occupying those dates is rejected at application preflight; an exclusive midnight end does not occupy the following day. Pending/rejected leave does not block scheduling. Later-approved leave is highlighted against an existing shift, without deleting either record. This is a scheduling check, not a database-wide guarantee against concurrent leave approval. Submitted recurring availability is enforced on new assignments; no submission means unknown availability. Staffing-demand targets, break rules and statutory scheduling policies are not inferred. Save shift publishes immediately; Save draft keeps a new shift private. Planned hours remain separate from actual punches and payroll.

## Researched planning and actual-hour review

New private drafts are hidden from employee schedules, Today and calendar downloads. Existing published shifts stay published when edited. Publishing validates the displayed draft set and all current conflicts atomically, respects the employee filter and sends one in-app notice per affected person. Copy previous week validates both source and target snapshots, keeps local clock times, and creates only drafts. No staff notice is sent until publication. Drafts reserve their employee's interval under the existing database overlap constraint; they cannot overlap another draft or published shift.

Employees own a versioned seven-day availability profile with any-time, unavailable or one contiguous available window per date. Midnight ends a day; overnight availability occupies both dates. A change flags existing conflicts and notifies managers without cancelling shifts. No-op submissions send no notices. Planning cost is a Decimal projection for the displayed local week, including drafts, at current profile rates; overtime estimates use the configured weekly multiplier and exclude other stores and payroll deductions.

Future published shifts can be offered only by their assigned employee. One coworker can request coverage at a time. Independent manager approval revalidates availability, approved leave, active profiles and overlapping shifts before transferring the assignment. Neither the original nor replacement employee may approve their own transfer. Declining reopens the offer; withdrawal retains the original assignment. Shift edits/cancellation close outstanding offers. The coverage board is store-scoped and paginated at 30 offers; it deliberately shares offered shifts, not coworkers' complete schedules. Two-way trades and unassigned open shifts are not implemented.

Personal calendar downloads include only the authenticated employee's published shifts, require sign-in, use UTC event times and stable scoped IDs, escape/fold text and disable caching. They are snapshots rather than a live subscription and do not remove previously imported cancelled events automatically.

Clock-hours review measures completed UTC intervals, splits at local midnight, and rounds once after each day's sum. It compares planned versus actual hours but never caps actual hours to the schedule or subtracts breaks automatically. Confirmation of payable work/breaks is required. Open punches, future work, approved/imported/voided payroll and differing manual totals block preparation. Existing notes and rate snapshots are retained. Preparation creates pending records, not approved wages or payments. Current source/target fingerprints reject stale previews; later punch edits flag sourced entries and prevent approval until reconciled. Manual corrections explicitly clear the source link and remain audited. Ordinary employees can inspect only their own preview and cannot prepare records.

SQLite command writers are serialized before snapshot validation; competing claims, publications and preparations cannot both commit the same displayed action. Version checks, active-offer uniqueness and transactional notices/audits reinforce that rule. A busy writer returns a reviewable conflict. New write limits apply per account: availability/publication/preparation 10 per minute, week copying 5, offers 10, claims/withdrawals 20 and coverage decisions 30. Read-only availability/clock previews do not consume write allowances.

CSV/PDF exports identify manual, imported and clock-derived hours and flag changed clock sources even if a row was approved earlier. They retain the historical approval status and make the current reconciliation need explicit. Source validation batches the selected employees/workweeks, with employee-ID query chunks of 500. Neither an approved status nor a download certifies that wages were paid.

Team chat uses separate channels shared by active employee-linked accounts within this installation, including all staff roles. Only administrators and executives create channels; all coworkers can read and post in each channel. Channel names are unique and use 1–40 lowercase letters, digits, or hyphens. Authors are always the authenticated user. Bodies are plain text of up to 2,000 characters, safely escaped in server-rendered HTML and inserted with textContent during polling. A message needs text or a valid picture. Sending requires CSRF and is limited to 30 messages per minute per user. Views show the most recent 25 messages initially, support loading earlier messages, and retrieve up to 100 per polling request. Channel filters apply to current, older, and new messages.

One picture per message is supported, with a 5 MB upload limit and 12 million source-pixel limit. JPG, PNG, WebP, and GIF are decoded and verified, resized to a longest edge of 2,048 pixels, stripped of original metadata, and re-encoded as JPEG or PNG. GIF is a still frame. Invalid pictures reject the entire message. Pictures and messages commit together and remain in SQLite, so verified database backups cover both. Serving pictures requires an authenticated active coworker profile, uses a fixed image MIME type, and sends no-store/nosniff headers. Private groups, arbitrary file attachments, direct messages, moderation, and retention controls are not implemented.

# Reviewed production policy and notifications

Payroll approval/void actions require the displayed version and cannot change the actor's own entry. Payroll dates and report periods follow the selected store's calendar; IDs are record IDs, never pagination-clamped values. Exports disclose estimate/approval/import status, and rejected/voided rows do not contribute to active totals.

Privileged production accounts require encrypted authenticator MFA. Ordinary and remembered sessions use revocable random account identities; password/role changes revoke them and discard unfinished enrollment. Recovery links are private, single-use, store/account-bound and valid for 30 minutes. Owner actions in a store confirm the hub password/MFA, and hub credential revocation invalidates issued store-owner sessions. Store owners can recover staff passwords through the app without disabling enabled MFA; lost owner authenticators require trusted identity-verified CLI recovery.

New, edited, reassigned and cancelled shifts send private in-app notices in the same transaction as the saved change. A rejected group creates no notices. An unchanged save creates no extra notice. Reassignment notifies both affected people; cancellation/reassignment links use the affected date's week. The notification button is visible in the header. No push, SMS or email delivery is implied.

Production wage approvals require a human review with an evidence reference for a bounded Virginia policy: 2026-2028 work dates, adult hourly nonexempt staff at one location for the employer, one rate, Monday-Sunday workweeks and weekly overtime. Imported and mixed-rate workweeks are blocked. Cross-location overtime, tips, bonuses, deductions, taxes and payments need the external payroll process. Eligibility is the owner's attestation: the app does not classify employees or detect work across separate store databases, and the stored review has no automatic expiry. Owners must keep the attested scope current and use external payroll for staff who move across locations. Recording the policy does not reconcile historical source rows or certify compliance. Effective minimum-rate boundaries were checked against [Virginia Code section 40.1-28.10](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title40.1/chapter3/section40.1-28.10/); work/record review follows the employer's process and [DOL recordkeeping guidance](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/21-flsa-recordkeeping).
